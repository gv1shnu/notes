The problem

Beginners in cybersecurity drown in tools and buzzwords, and in "free" resources that turn out to be paywalled. Nobody hands them a map: what to learn, why, where to learn it free, where to practise legally, and what's next.

I wanted a free-only, static learning platform that works like a small operating system for the field: a concept graph, a path through it, quizzes, progress tracking, all of it running in the browser with no account.

What's in the box

What I observed

  • "Free" is not binary. I needed a status enum: FREE, FREE_WITH_ACCOUNT, FREE_TIER, SELF_HOSTED_FREE, UNKNOWN, PAID, DEAD, plus a confidence grade (A–D) and a lastVerified date.
  • A concept page is only useful if it answers the same questions every time: summary, why it matters, mental model, offensive view, defensive view, CTF angle, bug-bounty angle, common mistakes, what's next.
  • Hand-curating practice rooms doesn't scale. An open MIT-licensed catalogue of TryHackMe rooms could be matched to concepts by topic + difficulty.
  • Hand-written JSON drifts: dangling prerequisite ids, duplicate URLs. It needs a validator.

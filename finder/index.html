The problem

I know what a file is ("the photo of kids playing in the rain", "my 2025 expense tracker") but not where it lives or what I named it. And I wanted to drive my desktop in plain English, from the terminal, entirely offline.

The brief: natural-language prompts in a Windows/Mac terminal; four flows (find image by content, find document by content, open an app, create a VeraCrypt container); completely local, iterative yet efficient. Then it grew past the four.

finder "find me the image with kids playing in rain" finder "find the document with my 2025 expense tracker" finder "open up valorant" finder "create a new veracrypt container with 50gb, ask me for the password" finder "find my photoshoot from jan 2026, and open paint" # several actions finder "zip all the pdfs in my Downloads into archive.zip" # anything else

The idea: the model plans, plain code acts

What I observed

  • The four flows are too varied for regex ("50gb", "expense tracker of 2025", "kids playing in rain"): an LLM is the only robust offline parser.
  • CLIP would mean torch (heavy, no new-Python wheels). But a local vision model can see an image and caption it, so I caption at index time and embed the caption, no extra ML deps.
  • A vision model given only pixels hallucinates identities: it captioned a photo from the palaktiwarii account as "Kiara Advani". The filename carries a reliable signal.
  • A bigger model doesn't fix niche recognition: a game-character render stays unidentified even on qwen2.5vl:32b, which answers "Unsure". That's a training-data ceiling, not a size one.
  • Users don't ask one thing at a time, and they ask for things off the menu. Hence plans, and a general confirm-then-run shell action.

the anti-hallucination rule

The captioner may name a real person from a handle, or a known fictional character, but must not invent a celebrity from looks alone — it describes generically when unsure. finder refuses to guess.
offline at runtime: the only connection is to localhost:11434 (Ollama).

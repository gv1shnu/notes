The problem

A timed SQL quiz for the DBMS lab where the timer and the score cannot be cheated: no restarting the clock with a refresh, no peeking at answers before the round closes, no replaying a finished run.

Instructors approve a question count and seconds-per-question, release one shared set, and review every student's results after closure. Students join with a Google account and get one resumable attempt. A playful touch: good/evil Jerry tempters at the halfway mark.

The thing that makes it fair

What shaped it

  • Every clock is the server's. A refresh re-reads the same deadline; disconnecting doesn't pause anything; reconnecting catches up, it doesn't grant extra time.
  • Everyone gets the same frozen set, but with per-student question order and opaque option tokens, so answers can't be shared by position.
  • Nothing is revealed early. Correct answers, explanations and scores stay sealed until the release ends, even for early finishers.
  • Submissions are immutable + idempotent. A duplicate can't change an accepted answer or double-score; the client safely retries uncertain submissions.
  • Clock skew is real. RPCs rejected for a future-issued token are retried (migration + commit for it).

roles by sign-in

Any verified Google account can play. Instructor access comes only from an email allowlist (public.instructor_emails), checked server-side; the instructor entry point is hidden from students.

The idea

An app that can't speak in its own voice. You type a message; it answers by splicing together fragments of dialogue from your own films and TV — the way Bumblebee talks through his radio after his voice box is destroyed.

Half the point is that it's approximate: it reaches for the nearest phrase it has, stitches the borrowed voices together with tuning static, and runs everything through one band-limited filter so a dozen unrelated recordings sound like a single damaged speaker.

Two loops and one boundary

The decisions that make it sound right

  • The band-pass is never optional. That single ~1150 Hz filter is what turns a playlist of unrelated clips into "one damaged speaker" — it's the whole aesthetic in one line.
  • Match by meaning, not keywords. Distinct phrases are embedded with nomic-embed-text, so a reply can reach for the nearest phrase it actually has.
  • Viterbi, not greedy. A cost-based matcher covers the text optimally with available clips, instead of grabbing the first match and getting stuck.
  • Seeded randomness. The same reply reproduces the same audio exactly — which is also how Export re-renders it offline to a WAV + matching SRT.
  • Never answer with silence; cap generation so it can't run away; leave a few dB of variance between sources so it sounds found, not produced.

everything local, your media only

The corpus is built entirely from your own files, lives in SQLite under userData, and is never bundled or distributed. Nothing leaves the machine except model downloads you ask for.
whisper covers English; a macOS on-device Speech second pass catches what it missed (often non-English).

The problem

An AI ecosystem sandbox, not a game you play. Two teams of autonomous agents raid a raptor nest to steal eggs and carry them home, while raptors — hostile to both teams — hunt whoever is most isolated. A human watches; a human never plays.

The slogan, straight from the design doc: the game is the environment, the AI is the product. It exists to answer research questions about agent behaviour.

The three-way standoff

The four research questions

  • Q1 (primary): do weight-space personalities produce measurably distinct behaviour? Preliminary: yes — after a fix.
  • Q3: how does a neutral hostile population change competitive equilibria?
  • Q4: does objective-gated sustain self-balance?
  • Q2: constrained communication under partial observability.

order is a dependency chain, not a preference

Q2 (varying radio vocabulary) would contaminate Q1's personality clustering, and Q1 is load-bearing for Q3/Q4 — "strategy distributions" over identical clones are meaningless. So: Q1 → Q3 → Q4 → Q2.

The most important finding (about the sim, not the agents)

Before headless batch mode existed, the sim was "verified" by watching it. The first real batch run exposed the truth:

=== 5 matches, 300s timer === eggs delivered 0.00 per match end reasons timeout 5

Personality had no leverage (a cautious and a reckless agent played identically) and the objective was broken (nobody ever delivered an egg). Both were fixed by giving each trait a primary action it owns outright, and pricing combat against the local force ratio.

you can't see a statistical bug by watching five matches — you measure it.

what each trait owns

aggression → Engage/Hunt · greed → Grab/Deliver/Intercept · caution → Retreat/Flee · teamplay → Regroup/Escort · patience → Hold

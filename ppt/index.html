The problem

I make a lot of teaching decks. Writing the words is the slow part, but every "AI slide maker" I tried either needed the cloud or produced slides with text spilling out of boxes.

What I wanted: write a short brief ("open with why indexes matter, compare a scan vs a lookup, show CREATE INDEX, close with a summary"), and get a .pptx that looks designed, generated on my own laptop, with nothing leaving it.

What I observed

  • A 3B model on an 8 GB M1 can write good bullets, but asking it for a whole deck as one JSON truncates and silently drops slides.
  • Small models love to label most slides "section", so you get a wall of title-only slides.
  • They wrap words in `backticks` and **bold**, which render literally on a slide.
  • They mislabel a code slide as "content", so the code silently disappears.
  • Generated decks "look off" mostly because text overflows its box, not because of colours.

the one rule for laptops

The model must fit in RAM with room to spare. An 8B model "fits" by weight on 8 GB, but with ~7 GB already used by the OS it swaps and drops to ~2.5 tok/s, so a whole-deck request times out. Below 16 GB, stay at 3–4B.
qwen2.5:3b ≈ 3.7 GB, ~12 tok/s, unusually clean JSON → default.
